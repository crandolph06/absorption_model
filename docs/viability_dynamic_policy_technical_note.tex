\title{\textbf{Policy Search for Pilot-Production Viability}}
\author{Tyler Korenyi-Both}
\date{June 2026}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
This note studies whether bounded pilot-production policies can produce a
3500-pilot force while satisfying wingman (WG), flight-lead (FL), and
instructor-pilot (IP) ready-aircrew-program (RAP) requirements. The policy
search is framed as a finite-horizon, open-loop control problem over a
20-year simulation. Candidate policies are generated with Sobol sampling,
heuristic seeds, and Gaussian-process surrogate screening, but feasibility
claims are made only after direct evaluation with the physics-backed simulator.
The current-bound closeout bundle contains 2158 successful direct-physics
evaluation rows across three- and five-epoch policy classes; no feasible policy
was found. The best observed current-bound schedule was a three-epoch
policy with $\phi=5.83075$, requiring
simultaneous relaxation of the total-pilot window, WG RAP, and FL RAP
constraints. A seed-controlled input-bound study found that one-at-a-time
relaxation of retention, intake, UTE, PAA, manning, FLUG, or IPUG bounds did
not produce feasibility; the best such counterfactual reduced $\phi$ to 5.33
but still violated both inventory timing and RAP requirements. A subsequent
joint widened-bound refinement, with annual intake, retention, UTE, and PAA
bounds relaxed together, improved the nearest miss to $\phi=5.63722$ but still
found no feasible schedule. These results are not a proof of infeasibility, but
they very strongly suggest with the current pilot production model, it is not
possible to meet both pilot headcount and readiness goals.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

We explore whether a policy exists that can generate the required
pilot inventory and keep the force trained enough to meet RAP requirements.
For this analysis, the inventory target is 3500 total pilots, and the enabled
RAP constraints require zero positive WG, FL, and IP RAP shortfall during the
post-assessment window.

A few terms are used throughout the note. WG, FL, and IP denote wingman,
flight-lead, and instructor-pilot categories. RAP is the ready aircrew program,
so a positive RAP shortfall means the simulated force lacks enough training
capacity or qualified personnel to satisfy that category's configured
requirement. UTE is aircraft utilization. PAA is primary aircraft authorization.
FLUG and IPUG are per-phase flight-lead and instructor-pilot upgrade quotas.
The assessment window begins in 2040 in this 2026--2045 simulation; results in
that window, not only final-year values, determine feasibility.

The policy levers are annual B-course intake, retention rate, utilization
(UTE), primary aircraft authorization (PAA), maximum manning percentage, FLUG
quota per phase, and IPUG quota per phase. A policy can be static, with one
value for each lever, or dynamic, with different values applied over different
parts of the 20-year horizon. The dynamic policies studied here are structured
open-loop schedules: the horizon is divided into a small number of contiguous
epochs, and each epoch holds the seven policy levers fixed.

The search uses a two-level evidence hierarchy. Surrogates are used to explore
the policy space, rank candidates, and focus direct evaluations near promising
regions. Direct ''physics'' (or running the simulator directly) remains the authority for all feasibility statements.
Sobol sequences are used for the main design-of-experiments steps because they
are deterministic low-discrepancy sequences that cover bounded
high-dimensional spaces more evenly than naive random sampling at the same
budget. The workflow is summarized in Fig.~\ref{fig:workflow}.

\section{Problem Formulation}

Let $x_k$ denote the compressed force and training state at simulation phase
$k$. The simulator state includes quantities such as total pilots, line pilots,
qualification mix, staff counts, upgrade burden, and RAP shortfalls. Let $u_e$
denote the policy controls assigned to epoch $e$:
\[
u_e =
\begin{bmatrix}
\text{intake}_e &
\text{retention}_e &
\text{UTE}_e &
\text{PAA}_e &
\text{manning}_e &
\text{FLUG}_e &
\text{IPUG}_e
\end{bmatrix}^{T}.
\]
The 20-year horizon contains 60 simulation phases. An $E$-epoch policy divides
those 60 phases into $E$ contiguous blocks. Within each block, the same seven
controls are applied at every phase. Thus, a three-epoch policy has 21 decision
variables, and each epoch is roughly one third of the 20-year horizon. An arbitrary
number of epochs could be used for an arbitrarily complex policy schedule.

The physics-backed simulator defines the nonlinear transition map
\[
  x_{k+1} = f_k(x_k,u_{e(k)}),
\]
where $e(k)$ maps phase $k$ to its active epoch. The search minimizes the
maximum normalized constraint violation,
\[
  \min_U \ \phi(U)
  = \min_U \ \max_i \frac{g_i(x_{0:N};U)}{s_i},
\]
where $g_i \le 0$ is the satisfied convention and $s_i$ is the configured
constraint scale. A policy is direct-feasible only if the direct physics
evaluation returns $\phi \le 0$. The boundary $\phi=0$ is exactly active
feasibility; positive $\phi$ is normalized violation.

The total-pilot window constraint is
\[
  g_{\mathrm{window}} =
  3500 - \min_{k \in \mathcal{A}} \mathrm{total\_pilots}_k ,
\]
where $\mathcal{A}$ is the post-assessment window, beginning at the configured
assessment year 2040. Therefore,
\texttt{total\_pilots\_window}=533 means the force falls to 2967 total pilots
at the worst point in the assessment window. It does not necessarily mean the
final force is short by 533 pilots.

\section{Search Campaign}

The main campaign used the physics-backed configuration in
\path{outputs/viability/dynamic_policy_search/config_physics.yaml}. The
three-epoch search evaluated structured heuristic seeds and a Sobol initial
design, fitted a Matern GPR to direct-physics $\phi$ values, screened a large
Sobol candidate pool with a lower-confidence-bound rule, and direct-verified
the top candidates. A targeted refinement then sampled and screened local
perturbations around the best direct schedules. A five-epoch search tested
whether a finer open-loop schedule changed the trade space. It did not improve
the best $\phi$, so the planned 10-epoch expansion was not run.

The IPUG row in Table~\ref{tab:best-policy} does not mean the simulated force
has no instructor pilots. With IPUG quota set to zero in all epochs, the best
policy still ends with 71 staff IPs, has a minimum post-assessment staff-IP
count of 58, and has IP RAP slack of 5.15. Figure~\ref{fig:staff-counts} shows
the same point over the full horizon. The optimizer is effectively using
existing IP inventory and pipeline dynamics while avoiding the FL RAP penalty
associated with additional IP upgrades.

The active-constraint distribution reinforces this joint-trade interpretation.
Across the 2158 current-bound closeout evaluation rows, the active constraint
was FL RAP 1128 times, WG RAP 731 times, and total-pilot window 299 times. Each
core constraint was satisfied by at least one policy somewhere in the search,
but the search did not find a policy satisfying the inventory window, WG RAP,
and FL RAP simultaneously.

The IPUG-specific counterfactual made the interpretation sharper. Holding the

\section{Joint Input-Bound Reoptimization Check}

The one-at-a-time bound study is intentionally local, so a final targeted check
jointly widened the most relevant upper bounds and reran the dynamic search
machinery. The widened configuration set annual intake high to 400, retention
high to 0.85, UTE high to 25, and PAA high to 40. Maximum manning, FLUG, and
IPUG bounds were left at their current values because the preceding diagnostics
did not identify them as promising first-order relaxations.

Two runs were made under the joint-bound configuration. The first was a fresh
three-epoch dynamic search with 519 initial direct evaluations and 128
optimizer-selected direct verifications. The second was an anchored refinement
from the current-bound best schedule, with 128 new direct verifications and the
previous 871-row current-bound refinement bundle retained for comparison. This
is still observed-search evidence, not a proof over the widened design space.

\section{Limitations and Recommendations}

The results in this report depend on the pilot manning simulation model.
It covers structured three- and five-epoch open-loop schedules under the bounds
in Table~\ref{tab:policy-bounds}. It does not cover fully free per-phase
controls, closed-loop feedback, or an exhaustive widened-bound optimization.
The one-at-a-time input-bound study shows that no single tested bound change
solved the problem around the current best schedule. The targeted joint-bound
check in Table~\ref{tab:joint-bound} shows that widening the most relevant
input bounds together improved the nearest miss but still did not produce
feasibility. In other words, this analysis cannot guarantee that no feasible
policy exists under the current model, but the evidence suggests that such a
policy is exceedingly unlikely.

The decision implications are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item If policy input bounds remain fixed, the best observed requirements
  trade starts from 533 pilots in the assessment window, 5.83075 WG RAP
  shortfall, and 2.94844 FL RAP shortfall.
  \item If requirements remain fixed, the completed joint-bound check suggests
  that modest widening of intake, retention, UTE, and PAA is insufficient by
  itself. Any further input-bound search should either broaden the bound set or
  increase the policy-class richness, rather than repeat another one-at-a-time
  sweep.
  \item If neither requirements nor input bounds can change, the next justified
  search class is richer dynamic policy structure. A fully free per-phase
  schedule should be considered only after deciding that the added complexity
  is worth the interpretability cost.
  \item The dashboard and report should center the direct-physics dynamic
  result bundle, nearest misses, and relaxation tables. Legacy static sliders
  should remain secondary screening tools.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}

Under the current tested policy bounds and structured open-loop policy classes,
the direct-physics search did not find a feasible policy. The nearest miss is
clear: the force eventually exceeds 3500 pilots, but not soon enough in the
assessment window, and WG/FL RAP constraints remain positive. The first
input-bound relaxation study did not reveal a single simple bound change that
creates feasibility. The targeted joint widened-bound refinement improved the
nearest miss but still did not create feasibility. The evidence therefore
points to a joint trade: relax the inventory/RAP requirements, broaden the
input-bound or policy-class search beyond the targeted check here, or expand
the dynamic policy class with a clear reason for the added complexity.
